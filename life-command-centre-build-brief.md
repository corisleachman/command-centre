# Life Command Centre

## Detailed product and build brief

**Working title:** Life Command Centre  
**Product type:** Private, single-user responsive web application  
**Primary user:** Coris  
**Initial planning period:** 1 August 2026 to 30 October 2026  
**Core purpose:** Convert long-term personal and commercial ambitions into a small, controlled set of daily actions, then visibly connect completed effort to commercial, health and life outcomes.

---

## 1. Product vision

Build a calm, visual personal operating system for someone with ADHD who is balancing urgent income generation, consultancy development, several software products, family finances and chronic health management.

This must not feel like a conventional project-management tool with an intimidating backlog. Strategy should sit underneath the interface. The surface should show only what needs attention now.

The product must answer five questions quickly:

1. What are the three things I need to do today?
2. Which one should I do first?
3. Am I doing enough of the activities most likely to produce income?
4. Are those activities creating measurable results?
5. Am I staying focused on the agreed plan rather than activating new ideas?

The primary success criterion is behavioural. The user should be able to open the app each morning, understand the day in under 30 seconds, start the highest-value task, and finish the day without browsing a large backlog.

## 2. Personal and strategic context

The user is about to turn 48 and has recently finished a contract as Executive Director of Growth at Jungle Creations. He is currently without a secure income and is the main income provider for a family of five. One daughter is about to begin university, with further education costs likely for another child within two or three years.

The long-term target is to generate between £150,000 and £250,000 a year, with £200,000 as the central planning target. This should ultimately come from several connected revenue streams rather than a single employer:

- Agency growth and new-business consultancy
- Fractional growth leadership
- A productised agency growth dashboard and advisory offer
- Software subscription income
- Workshops and advisory projects

The user has three existing commercial assets:

- An agency growth and new-business consultancy proposition
- An agency growth dashboard concept, based on experience gained at Jungle but to be rebuilt independently without using Jungle’s confidential code, data, terminology or intellectual property
- Pulse Outreach, an outreach application
- The Song Room, a collaboration application for musicians, producers and bands

The strategic order for the initial 90 days is:

1. **Cash objective:** Sell consultancy, fractional leadership, diagnostics and the productised growth dashboard.
2. **Product experiment:** Run a paid validation test for The Song Room.
3. **Health objective:** Establish a sustainable movement, strength and energy routine compatible with CIDP and clinical advice.
4. **Maintenance only:** Use Pulse for the user’s own outreach where helpful, but do not make it an active product-development priority.
5. **Safety net:** Continue selected permanent, interim or fractional job applications until recurring income is secure.

The user has ADHD and is visually oriented. Large blocks of text encourage skimming and disengagement. The interface must use progressive disclosure, visual cards, short labels, strong hierarchy and clear status. Detail should be available through drawers, modals or dedicated detail pages, but should not dominate the home screen.

## 3. Product principles

These principles are requirements, not general aspirations.

### 3.1 Action on the surface, strategy underneath

The default view shows current action. Long-term plans, full activation plans and the backlog are available, but are never the first screen.

### 3.2 One-one-one rule

At any time, there can be only:

- One primary cash objective
- One active product experiment
- One primary health focus

The system must visibly warn the user if they try to activate more.

### 3.3 Today is deliberately scarce

The standard daily commitment is exactly three Big Three tasks:

- One **Cash Now** task
- One **Build** task
- One **Health** or **Life** task

The app can show appointments and short follow-ups separately, but it must not turn the Today screen into a long task list.

### 3.4 Tasks must be genuinely small

Every actionable task should normally take between 10 and 90 minutes. Tasks estimated above 90 minutes must display a warning and offer to split them into subtasks.

### 3.5 Effort and results are different

The dashboard must show leading activity indicators separately from outcomes. Completing easy administrative work must not create the illusion of commercial progress.

### 3.6 New ideas do not become work automatically

Every screen should offer quick idea capture. New ideas go into the Idea Car Park with a default status of **Not now**. They can only become active during a weekly or monthly review.

### 3.7 Carry-over is a decision

An incomplete task must never silently roll into the next day. The user must explicitly reschedule, split, delegate, defer or cancel it, with an optional short reason.

### 3.8 Energy matters

CIDP and general health can affect capacity. The app should support low, medium and high capacity days without turning a low-energy day into a failure.

### 3.9 Calm, not punitive

Use neutral language. Avoid red screens, shame-based streaks and aggressive productivity language. Red should mean strategic risk, not personal failure.

- 1 reusable proposal template

### Objective B: Song Room Paid Validation

**Outcome statement:** Determine whether active musicians, producers or bands will pay monthly to manage versions, feedback and decisions in one shared workspace.

**90-day targets:**

- 20 target-user interviews
- 10 active trial workspaces
- 5 paying customers
- A founding-member price tested

- Three planned movement or strength sessions in most weeks, subject to clinical advice and symptoms
- Record a simple daily energy score
- Complete one weekly health reflection
- Keep relevant clinical appointments and actions
- Create a low-capacity-day task mode

This product must not provide medical prescriptions. Health tasks are entered by the user and should be aligned with advice from relevant clinicians or physiotherapists.

## 10. Screen specifications

### 10.1 Today

This is the default route after login.

Header:

- Greeting using the user’s first name
- Full date
- “Week X of 13”
- Small capacity selector: Low, Standard or High
- Current weekly points, shown discreetly

Primary section:

- Three large Big Three cards
- First card must normally be Cash Now
- Each card shows:
  - Short task title
  - Category icon and colour
  - Estimated time
  - Points
  - Linked objective
  - Start focus button
  - Complete button
  - Overflow menu: edit, split, reschedule, defer, cancel

If the Big Three have not been chosen, show a guided setup that recommends three tasks based on priority, due date, category balance, objective health and capacity.

Secondary compact cards:

- Appointments today
- Follow-ups due
- Current focus-session timer
- Energy check-in

Bottom actions:

- Capture an idea
- Record a result
- End the day

The Today screen must not display the full backlog.

### 10.2 Focus mode

Opening a task creates a distraction-minimised screen:

- One task only
- Why it matters
- Definition of done
- Estimated duration
- Start, pause and finish timer
- Optional checklist
- Notes field
- “I’m stuck” action

“I’m stuck” should offer:

- Make the first step smaller
- Split the task
- Switch to a low-energy version
- Reschedule with a reason

On completion, ask only relevant questions:

- What happened?
- Did this create a measurable activity or result?
- Optional follow-up task

### 10.3 End of day

Use a five-minute guided flow:

1. Review the Big Three
2. Resolve each incomplete task
3. Record outcomes produced
4. Enter energy score from 1 to 5
5. Enter a one-line note: “What moved today?”
6. Suggest tomorrow’s first task

Show a short, neutral summary such as:

> You completed 2 of 3 priorities, created one sales conversation and protected your health session.

### 10.4 Week

This is the main planning board.

Provide two switchable views:

- **Commitment board:** This Week, Today, In Progress, Waiting, Completed
- **Calendar view:** Monday to Sunday with time-block suggestions

Requirements:

- Drag and drop on desktop
- Simple move controls on mobile
- Filter by objective, plan, category and owner
- Show planned points and category allocation
- Warn if:
  - More than 15 meaningful tasks are committed
  - Cash Now is below 40% of planned effort
  - More than one product experiment is active
  - A task is over 90 minutes
  - Too many tasks are assigned to one day

Show a weekly balance visual:

- Cash Now target: 50%
- Build target: 25%
- Health target: 15%
- Life/Admin target: 10%

These percentages should be editable in Settings.

### 10.5 Objectives

Default to the current 90-day cycle.

Display exactly three prominent objective cards:

- Cash Engine
- Song Room Paid Validation
- Health and Operating Capacity

Each card shows:

- Outcome statement
- Current status
- Days remaining
- Target and actual metrics
- Progress bar
- Confidence
- Linked plans
- Next milestone
- “Open detail”

Below the active objectives, show a clearly separated Not Now area containing Pulse and any future initiatives.

Add a secondary Horizons tab for six-month, one-year, three-year, five-year and ten-year outcomes.

### 10.6 Plan detail

Each activation plan contains:

- Short strategy summary
- Workstreams
- Milestones
- Task backlog
- Metrics
- Risks and assumptions
- Decisions
- Linked resources

Default to a collapsed workstream view. Let the user expand one workstream at a time.

The task generator can suggest micro-tasks from a milestone, but the user must approve them before they enter the weekly plan.

### 10.7 Scorecard

Separate **Effort** from **Results**.

Effort metrics:

- Personal outreach messages sent
- Sales or research conversations requested
- Conversations booked
- Conversations completed
- Proposals sent
- Product demonstrations completed
- Song Room interviews completed
- Active trial workspaces
- Focus sessions completed
- Health or movement sessions completed

Results metrics:

- Contracted revenue
- Cash collected
- Qualified pipeline value
- Monthly recurring revenue
- Paying product users
- Proposal conversion rate
- Household runway in months
- Weekly average energy

Show:

- This week versus target
- Last four weeks
- Current 90-day total versus target
- A conversion funnel:
  - Contacts
  - Replies
  - Conversations
  - Proposals
  - Wins
  - Contracted value
- A short effort-to-outcome observation generated using deterministic rules in version one

Example:

> Outreach volume increased, but conversations did not. Review message quality and target fit before increasing volume again.

### 10.8 Reviews

Review types:

- End of day
- Weekly review
- Monthly review
- 90-day review

Weekly review flow:

1. What did I commit to?
2. What did I complete?
3. What measurable results occurred?
4. Which activities created movement?
5. What was avoided or repeatedly carried over?
6. What should stop, continue or change?
7. Resolve waiting and overdue tasks.
8. Review Idea Car Park without activating ideas by default.
9. Choose next week’s maximum 15 commitments.
10. Select Monday’s first Cash Now task.

Monthly review flow:

1. Review life-area confidence.
2. Review runway and financial position.
3. Review each 90-day objective.
4. Reassess the continue, pause or stop status of products.
5. Consider Idea Car Park items.
6. Approve any strategic change.

### 10.9 Idea Car Park

Idea cards show:

- Short title
- Capture date
- Optional notes
- Related life area or product
- Why it is tempting
- Review date
- Status: Not now, Reviewing, Activated, Archived

New ideas always default to Not now. Activating an idea requires:

- Selecting the objective it supports
- Explaining what existing work will be paused or removed
- Confirming that the one-one-one rule remains intact

### 10.10 Contacts and opportunities

Contacts can sit inside the Cash Engine plan rather than in the primary navigation.

Contact fields:

- Name
- Organisation
- Role
- Relationship type
- Warmth
- Email
- LinkedIn URL
- Last contact date
- Next action date
- Notes
- Consent or source notes where relevant

Opportunity fields:

- Organisation
- Contact
- Offer
- Stage
- Estimated value
- Probability
- Weighted value
- Expected close date
- Next action
- Next action date
- Source
- Lost reason

Stages:

- Identified
- Contacted
- Conversation booked
- Qualified
- Proposal
- Verbal agreement
- Won
- Lost
- Nurture

Qualified pipeline should include only opportunities at Qualified stage or later.

### 10.11 Finances

Keep version one deliberately simple and manually updated:

- Essential monthly household expenditure
- Accessible cash
- Confirmed personal income
- Business cash
- Contracted but unpaid revenue
- Estimated runway in months
- Tax pot
- University pot
- Holiday pot
- Emergency fund
- Monthly pension contribution

Runway calculation:

`(accessible personal cash + available business cash reserved for drawings) / essential monthly household expenditure`

Do not connect bank accounts in version one.

- Created source: manual, seeded, suggested

### Categories

- Cash Now
- Build
- Health
- Life
- Idea

### Task statuses

- Backlog
- This month
- This week
- Today
- In progress
- Waiting
- Completed
- Deferred
- Cancelled

### Recommendation score

Use a transparent deterministic score in version one:

- Revenue proximity: 0 to 5
- Impact: 1 to 5
- Urgency: 1 to 5
- Objective at risk: 0 to 3
- Overdue or due today: 0 to 3
- Carry-over penalty: minus 0 to 2 if repeated carry-over suggests the task needs splitting
- Capacity mismatch: minus 3 if task energy exceeds today’s selected capacity

Cash Now tasks receive a small tie-break advantage during the initial 90-day cycle.

The app should explain recommendations in plain language:

> Recommended first because it is revenue-related, due today and supports the Cash Engine target.

- Show contracted revenue and qualified pipeline beside points.

## 13. Capacity modes

### Low-capacity day

- Recommend one Cash Now task of 15 to 30 minutes
- Recommend one light Build or Life task
- Recommend one approved recovery, mobility or health action
- Avoid tasks marked high energy
- Allow the user to reduce the Big Three to a “Minimum Viable Day” without breaking a streak

### Standard day

- Standard Big Three
- Two focused work blocks
- Normal meetings and follow-ups

### High-capacity day

- Standard Big Three remains
- Additional tasks can appear only after the Big Three are complete
- Do not expand the initial morning list

- Metric entries store dated values.
- Opportunities link to contacts and can create metric entries.
- Reviews store structured answers and resulting decisions.

Use UUID primary keys, `created_at`, `updated_at`, `archived_at` where appropriate, and row-level security on every user-owned table.

Use database views or server-side queries for:

- Current weekly category allocation
- 90-day objective progress
- Funnel conversion
- Qualified pipeline
- Weighted pipeline
- Weekly points
- Runway
- Task carry-over count

## 17. Visual design direction

The interface should feel like a calm executive dashboard combined with a personal coach. It must not resemble Jira, a dense CRM or a childish habit tracker.

### Visual qualities

- Calm
- Premium
- Clear
- Optimistic
- Focused
- Visual before textual
- Warm enough to feel personal
- Serious enough to support commercial decisions

### Suggested palette

- Background: warm off-white or very pale stone
- Primary text: near-black navy or charcoal
- Cash Now: deep green
- Build: blue or indigo
- Health: muted coral or rose
- Life: warm amber
- Idea: violet
- Success: green
- Risk: muted red
- Cards: white with subtle border and shadow

Use colour as reinforcement, not as the only carrier of meaning.

### Typography and spacing

- Use a highly legible sans serif such as Geist or Inter.
- Use large numeric values for revenue, pipeline, users and runway.
- Keep card copy short.
- Use generous spacing.
- Avoid large text blocks on dashboards.
- Use drawers or detail pages for explanations.

### Motion

- Subtle progress transitions
- Gentle completion state
- No confetti by default
- No distracting continuous animation
